Why Homebrew worked

The name was part of the interface.

I named Homebrew before I wrote its code. I wanted the name to lead naturally to the rest of the product and make an obscure system easier to understand.

It started with the language.

A co-worker at Last.fm suggested beer. I chose Homebrew because people would create and customize their own packages. Formula beat recipe because it was more distinctive. Most of the language followed within a week.

The first public README already had Homebrew, a Brewery, a Cellar, formulae, kegs, and the brew command. Later I added brew doctor and taps.

“I wanted a theme that allowed people to see analogies and metaphors in all of the system and code.”

Four names. Four different jobs.

A name can explain the product, establish its character, make a category feel familiar, or create a language the company can keep using.

One letter, three readings

Automic Vault

I started with Atomic Vault: a safe place against the worst possible attack. One changed letter made it ownable. Automic can read as a novel abbreviation of automations or automatons while keeping the atomic threat imagery. Its vocabulary now includes isotopes, gates, hardening, and blessed scripts.

The pun became the product

Supermassive Book Hole

It came to me in the shower as a pun on Supermassive Black Hole. Then the product metaphor appeared: sucking books into one place where you can find loadsa info on them. The name explains the product and supplies its image in four words.

Familiar on purpose

PromiseKit

Bland by design. Promise says exactly what the framework implements. Kit copies Apple’s naming pattern so it already feels like a fundamental part of the ecosystem.

Literal until it becomes funny

Package Manager Manager

It manages package managers. The repetition is both the explanation and the joke. Then pkg⋅mgr² compresses the long name into a compact product mark.

Names are product decisions.

The cleverest answer is not always the right one. Sometimes the job is to create an image, inherit useful meaning, or name the behavior at the heart of the product.

Visual metaphor

Filelight

Give the product an image.

Filelight is KDE's disk usage visualizer. I was thinking about flashlight and firelight: revealing what is hidden, with an image of light the interface and identity could use.

Unlaunched concept

Podium

Name the social act.

Podium was a concept for agent-enabled developers to show their work to a feed of people eager to respond, with AI turning that feedback into the next iteration. The name puts presenting, being seen, and public response at the center.
